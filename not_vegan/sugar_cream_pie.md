NAME

Sugar Cream Pie with Pâte Brisée (All-Butter Sweet Pie Crust)

Servings

1 (9-inch) pie, approximately 8 servings

Prep Time

25 minutes, plus 1 hour chilling time for the dough

Cook Time

Approximately 40–50 minutes

Ingredients

Pâte Brisée — All-Butter Sweet Pie Dough

* 2 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
* 1 tsp salt
* 1 tsp sugar
* 1 cup cold butter, cut into small pieces
* 1/4–1/2 cup ice-cold water

Sugar Cream Filling

* 1 baked 9-inch pie crust
* 4 Tbsp cornstarch
* 3/4 cup white sugar
* 4 Tbsp butter, melted
* 2 1/4 cups half-and-half cream
* 1 tsp vanilla extract

Topping

* 2 Tbsp butter, melted
* 1/2 tsp ground cinnamon

Optional Egg Wash

* 1 egg
* 1 Tbsp water

Directions

Prepare the Pâte Brisée

1. In a large bowl, combine the flour, salt, and sugar.
2. Using a pastry blender, forks, or food processor, cut the cold butter into the flour mixture until it resembles coarse crumbs.
3. Gradually add the ice-cold water, mixing until the dough just comes together.
4. Divide the dough into two equal portions and form each into a disk.
5. Wrap each disk in plastic wrap or parchment paper and refrigerate for at least 1 hour.
6. The dough may be frozen for future use. Use within one month of freezing.
7. Roll out one chilled disk and fit it into a 9-inch pie plate. Reserve the second disk for another pie.

Blind Bake the Pie Crust

1. Preheat the oven to 425°F (220°C). Place a baking sheet in the oven to preheat.
2. Once the dough is chilled and firm, pierce the bottom and sides of the crust with a fork to prevent air pockets.
3. Line the crust with a double thickness of aluminum foil or a single layer of parchment paper, ensuring it reaches the edges of the pan.
4. Fill the lined crust with pie weights or dried beans, distributing them evenly across the bottom and partially up the sides.
5. Place the pie plate on the preheated baking sheet and reduce the oven temperature to 400°F (204°C).
6. Bake for 10–12 minutes, until the crust edges begin turning golden and the bottom loses its translucent, raw appearance.
7. Remove the baking sheet and pie plate from the oven. Carefully lift out the foil or parchment with the pie weights.
8. Return the crust and baking sheet to the oven.
9. For a crust that will be baked again with its filling, bake for an additional 6–8 minutes, until the bottom begins to develop color.
10. For this sugar cream pie, which requires a fully baked crust, continue baking until the entire crust is golden brown, approximately 15 additional minutes.
11. Remove the crust from the oven and allow it to cool completely before adding the filling.

Optional Egg Wash

1. For additional protection against a soggy crust, beat 1 egg with 1 Tbsp water.
2. After the initial 6–8 minutes of baking without pie weights, brush the inside of the crust with the egg wash.
3. Return the crust to the oven for approximately 3 minutes, until the egg wash is dry and shiny.
4. Continue baking as needed until the crust is fully golden brown.

Prepare the Sugar Cream Filling

1. In a saucepan, combine the cornstarch and white sugar.
2. Add 4 Tbsp melted butter and the half-and-half cream. Mix thoroughly.
3. Cook over medium heat, stirring constantly, until the mixture comes to a boil and becomes thick and creamy.
4. Continue cooking for 1 minute.
5. Remove from heat and stir in the vanilla extract.

Assemble and Finish the Pie

1. Preheat the oven broiler to high.
2. Pour the warm sugar cream filling into the fully baked pie crust.
3. Drizzle 2 Tbsp melted butter evenly over the filling.
4. Sprinkle the ground cinnamon over the top.
5. Place the pie under the broiler until the butter begins to bubble.
6. Watch carefully, as the topping can scorch quickly.
7. Remove the pie from the oven and allow it to cool.
8. Refrigerate for at least 1 hour before serving.

Notes

* The Pâte Brisée recipe produces enough dough for two single 9-inch pie crusts. Only one is needed for this pie.
* For cream pies and icebox pies, the crust should be fully baked and cooled before filling.
* For pies that return to the oven for baking, the crust may still be warm when the filling is added.
* Dried beans used as pie weights can be saved for future blind baking but should not be eaten after baking.
* Broiling times vary considerably. Watch the pie continuously to prevent scorching.
